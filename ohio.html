How ongoing care works

Most of the work happens between appointments.

Chronic conditions don't wait for your next opening. Here's the rhythm of care once you're established.

  1. First visit

    A full picture

    We review your history, medications and labs, then set a plan with specific targets — in the Canfield office or by video, whichever is easier.

  2. Between visits

    Numbers we can see

    Where it helps, you take blood pressure, weight or glucose readings at home on a device we provide. Your physician reviews them and reaches out when something needs attention.

  3. Every month

    Someone checking in

    Regular follow-up, refills before you run out, labs ordered on schedule, and specialist referrals coordinated for you. Covered by Medicare and most Ohio plans.

Good to know

Questions, answered.

Do you take my insurance?

In Ohio, most likely. We're in-network with Medicare, Ohio Medicaid and most major commercial plans, including Aetna, Anthem BC/BS, UnitedHealthcare, Humana, Cigna, Medical Mutual, CareSource and Buckeye, with Molina coming soon. Call or text and we'll verify your specific plan before you book.

I have Medicare — how does billing work?

We accept Medicare in Ohio, so covered visits are billed to Medicare and you pay your normal cost-sharing. We won't charge you a private rate for a service your plan covers. When you reach out, we'll walk you through exactly how your visit will be billed.

Can I be seen in person?

Yes. We see patients at our Canfield office at 3695 Boardman Canfield Dr, Building B, and by video anywhere in Ohio. Many patients do a mix of both — an in-person visit to get established, then video for routine follow-up.

How does the home monitoring work?

For conditions like hypertension and diabetes, we can provide a connected blood pressure cuff, scale or glucometer. Your readings come to us automatically, your physician reviews them, and we contact you if something needs adjusting. It's a covered benefit under Medicare and most Ohio plans.

How soon can I be seen?

Often the same or next day. Evening and weekend appointments are available. If the times listed don't work, reach out — we can usually arrange something.

Can you prescribe medication?

Yes, when it's clinically appropriate. After evaluating you, your physician can send prescriptions to your pharmacy. Certain medications carry additional rules and may require an in-person visit.
